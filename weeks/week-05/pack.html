The 70/20/10 content portfolio — stop reinventing your content every morning

Repeatable formats, one proven core, a measured mix — and one locked position on the compass. This is how daily volume stays high-quality.

The idea

Daily content from a blank page burns everyone out. The answer is formats — repeatable, recognizable templates where the structure stays the same and only the content inside changes. A good format passes three tests:

TestThe question
LegibilityDoes your audience instantly get what this is and why it's for them? A format they have to decode has already lost.
ReplicabilityCould you make this every day for months without getting sick of it? Structure constant, content variable.
PracticalityCan you afford it — time, money, location, energy? A format that needs a new set per video fails before the algorithm sees it.

Formats are found by testing, not taste. Post a candidate daily and within about two weeks you have a clear verdict. Get forensic about the winners — hook, topic, outfit, setting — and recreate them with slight variations. A format that consistently grades around 0.5 VTFR becomes your core format — and then you ride it until it dies. One humbling pattern from the course's own numbers: effort doesn't predict performance. Polished passion projects routinely lose to simple text-over-B-roll. That's why we test instead of guessing.

The portfolio

70%Core format — your proven winner, the engine of the account
20%Batchable filler — text over B-roll, trends adapted into your niche
10%Experiments — most fail; one becomes your next core

All three buckets stay 100% inside your niche. A trend is a delivery vehicle, never a detour: filter it through your niche's inside jokes so your audience feels it was made for them — while the algorithm still recognizes the trend. Make the universal feel specific.

The Content Compass — where your content sits, every time

Formats answer what you make. The compass answers how it lands. X-axis: value (educate, instruct, inform) vs. entertainment (make them feel something). Y-axis: attainable — you're on the viewer's level, relatable, "one of us"; fans buy on hope of connection — vs. aspirational — you're above them; authority, glamour, spectacle; fans buy on admiration. The hidden Z-axis is the specific emotion you evoke — it's why two creators in the same quadrant don't feel identical.

ValueEntertainment
AttainableBob Ross — expert, but "you can do this too"The funny friend — no lesson, you just love being around her
AspirationalGordon Ramsay — teaches from authority: "aspire to my standard"Beyoncé — pure spectacle; not on her level, can't look away
This week's play Pick your one quadrant with your manager and lock it — then every post comes from a named bucket (core / filler / experiment) and hits that same quadrant. Every single post. No quadrant is better — but the undifferentiated middle converts nobody.

Do / don't

Do

  • Keep the structure constant, vary the content inside it.
  • Retire a core format on the numbers (falling VTFR, HPF at zero) — never on boredom.
  • Go deeper in your quadrant via your signature emotion, not wider across the compass.

Don't

  • Post mostly experiments to "keep it fresh" — fresh is what the 10% is for.
  • Hop quadrants post to post — drill sergeant Monday, relatable Tuesday reads as nobody.
  • Copy a format your audience won't instantly understand, however well it performs elsewhere.

This week's homework

  1. List every format you post and score each 1–5 on legibility, replicability, practicality.
  2. With your ALTR manager: pull VTFR per format. Core candidate at ~0.5? Name it. None? Pick the strongest and test it daily for two weeks.
  3. Sort your last 20 posts onto the compass. Choose your one quadrant — and commit in writing.
