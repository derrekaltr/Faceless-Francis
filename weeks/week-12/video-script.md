# WK 12 Video Script — Edit for Retention + The Full Pipeline (Program Finale)

> Presenter: ALTR social media director · Audience: ALTR client (creator) watching
> Runtime target: ~3 min (≈480 words of dialogue at ~140 wpm)
> Setting: clean branded set (charcoal backdrop, mint accent) · Framing: eye level (relatable-expert)

## Production notes

- Teleprompter: yes. Dialogue is exactly what's spoken.
- Lower-third at 0:05: "[NAME] · Social Media Director, ALTR"
- `[GFX: …]` graphics cues; `[B-ROLL: …]` coverage cues. Not spoken.
- Edit per retention rules: re-hook every ~3s, captions on, first-frame motion.
- **Finale:** no next-week tease. Close on the program end-card ("The whole system — now we run it").

## Script

**COLD OPEN (hook, ≤5s)**

This week my editors took three minutes of a creator's raw footage — and threw away two minutes and fifteen seconds. On purpose. That was the most valuable work we did all week.

**INTRO**

I'm [NAME], your social media director at ALTR — and this is week twelve. The last one. Today: what actually happens to your content after you hit upload. And then we zoom out — because you've now seen the entire machine.

[GFX: "WK 12 — EDIT FOR RETENTION · THE FULL PIPELINE"]

**BODY**

First, where your clips go. Every creator here runs on five folders. The first one is yours: the Dump. The day you shoot, everything goes straight off your phone into it. No naming, no sorting, no judging. From there my team triages what gets edited now, what within three days, what within a week. Edited clips pass quality control, get scheduled, and get filed by the week they actually post — so we always know when a clip last ran.

[GFX: five folders appearing as a pipeline, Dump → Greatest Hits]

And then there's my favorite folder: Greatest Hits. Your proven winners. Roughly every thirty days, we post them again. Free growth from content that already paid for itself — and over time, that folder becomes a map of exactly what your audience wants. It's also why we never delete raw footage. Ever. The clip you hate today is next month's B-roll — or a brand-new post.

Now, the edit itself. The algorithm pays for one thing: retention — how many viewers make it to the end. So every clip passes a ten-point checklist. Motion in the very first frame, because a still frame gets swiped. A new reason to keep watching every three seconds. Every pause and every breath cut — that's how three minutes becomes forty-five seconds. Little zooms that make one phone look like a two-camera shoot. Captions always on, in your brand style, because most people watch muted. Every graphic earns its place and lands with a sound. And before anything ships, we listen three times: headphones, speakers, phone.

[B-ROLL: editing timeline — dead air being cut, captions popping in]

One more number. We cap most edits at an hour — because a strong seven-out-of-ten posted every day beats a perfect ten posted once a week. Every time.

[GFX: "7/10 DAILY beats 10/10 WEEKLY"]

And that's the machine. Twelve weeks ago we started with the algorithm — the matchmaker — and the three numbers we grade. Then how a stranger becomes a fan who pays. Then who that fan is: your niche, your formats, your brand. Then accounts that live, and a posting machine that never misses. Then hooks, endless ideas, shooting like a studio. And today — the edit that keeps every viewer, and the pipeline that runs it all.

[GFX: twelve-week ladder, WK 1 → WK 12]

**HOMEWORK + CLOSE**

Your last homework is the easiest and the most important: tonight, dump every raw clip from the last month into your Dump folder — especially the ones you almost deleted. That's your whole job in post-production. Deliver fast. Delete nothing. We do the rest.

[GFX: "HOMEWORK: dump everything — delete nothing"]

There's no next week to tease. This is the whole system — now we run it. Every week, every post, together. Thank you for twelve weeks. Let's get to work.
