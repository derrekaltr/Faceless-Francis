## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

See exactly what happens to every clip you shoot — the pipeline it moves through, the 10-point edit standard it must pass, and the recycling system that keeps your best content earning for you forever.

## The idea

The algorithm pays for exactly one thing: retention. Not beauty, not meaning, not effort — what percentage of viewers make it to the end. So everything our editors do to your footage is **retention editing**: every cut, caption, zoom, and sound is a deliberate choice that keeps a viewer around for a few seconds longer. Three minutes of raw footage goes in; about 45 seconds comes out. That's not waste — that's the job. Every pause, every breath, every moment of dead air gets cut, because even half a second of nothing tells a viewer "this part isn't for me."

Inside those 45 seconds, the edit re-hooks constantly: a new reason to keep watching roughly every 3 seconds — a cut, a text pop, a graphic, a sound effect, ideally several at once. Jump cuts get disguised as camera moves with alternating 10–20% punch-ins, so one phone looks like a two-camera shoot. B-roll keeps the frame changing even while the audio runs on. Captions are always on — roughly 85% of social video is watched muted — styled in your brand's locked font and colors, inside the center 80% of the frame so platform buttons never cover them. Every graphic has to justify its existence *and* carry a sound effect, because a visual with sound reads as real; without it, it floats. And the audio itself is mixed to spec: your voice loudest, music felt-not-heard, 3–5 sound effects per 30 seconds.

Just as important is the pipeline your clips live in. Each creator gets five folders: **Dump** (you drop everything straight off your phone — zero friction), **Needs Editing** (triaged: high = now, medium = 72 hours, low = a week), **Ready to Schedule**, **Scheduled** (filed by month and week of *posting date*, so we always know when a clip last ran), and **Greatest Hits**. That last folder is the quiet money-maker: proven winners get re-posted roughly every 30 days — free volume from content that already paid for itself — and over time the folder becomes a literal map of what your audience wants most. Which is also why we never delete raw footage: today's reject is next month's B-roll, hook test, or brand-new post.

One more honest number: our editors cap most edits at about an hour. The target is strong 7-out-of-10 content at scale — a full three-week batch of ~21 clips runs on roughly 40 working hours across scripting, shooting, and editing — because good content posted daily beats perfect content posted once a week, every time.

## This week's trick (the single imperative)

Your entire job in post-production is two sentences: get every raw clip into your Dump folder the same day you shoot it, and never delete anything. The pipeline does the rest.

## Do / don't

**Do:** dump raws the day you shoot — zero friction, straight from the phone · shoot a little extra around every clip (walk-ins, hands, objects — future B-roll is gold) · start moving before the camera does: motion in frame one is an edit we can't fake · trust the 7/10 bar — volume of good beats a trickle of perfect.
**Don't:** delete raw footage, ever — including the takes you hate · sit on footage waiting for it to feel perfect · judge an edit with sound on only — most of your audience will never hear it · re-post a winner early; the ~30-day cycle is what keeps it a winner.

## This week's homework (client actions)

1. Dump every raw clip sitting on your phone from the last month into your Dump folder — especially the ones you almost deleted.
2. With your ALTR manager: pick three Greatest Hits from your last 90 days and put them back on the schedule.
3. Watch your newest edited clip **muted**. If it still works with no sound, the edit is doing its job — and that's how most people will see it.
