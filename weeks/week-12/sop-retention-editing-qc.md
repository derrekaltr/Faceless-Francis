# SOP — Retention Editing QC (10-Point Checklist + Audio Specs)

> Program week: 12 · Owner: head editor · Cadence: per clip, before anything enters Ready to Schedule · Status: draft
> Fills gap in `weeks/week-12/gap-audit.md`. Destination: `altr-management/03-content-ops/organic-growth-program/`.

## Purpose

Converts "editor taste" into an enforceable standard. The algorithm pays for retention — the percentage of viewers who reach the end — so every clip ships only after passing the 10-point retention checklist, the caption/graphic standards, and the audio mix spec below. Adopted from course companion tool 25 (checklist PDF + CSV — print one per editor).

## Trigger

Every clip, every time, before the editor moves it to `03-Ready-to-Schedule/` (see `sop-five-folder-pipeline.md` step 4). No exceptions for "simple" trend clips — they take ~15 minutes to edit and still get the checklist.

## Procedure — the 10-point checklist

Work through all ten; a clip ships only at 10/10.

| # | Checkpoint | Why (the standard to judge against) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **First-frame motion** | The serve/no-serve decision happens in ~0.5 s. A static opening frame gets swiped. Motion in frame one — a walk-in, head turn, gesture, zoom — signals "something is happening" before the brain decides to scroll. |
| 2 | **3-second re-hooks** | Attention decays in ~3 s cycles; every flat 3-second stretch is an exit ramp. Map the timeline: a cut, text pop, graphic, or SFX — ideally several in tandem — at least every 3 s, start to finish. |
| 3 | **Dead air removal** | The #1 retention killer. Cut every pause, um, breath gap, and transition lull; fill unavoidable gaps with music/SFX; speed-ramp slow sections. Benchmark: ~3 min raw → ~45 s posted. Every second has words, visuals, or something happening. |
| 4 | **Punch-ins** | Alternate 10–20% zooms on jump cuts (wide → punched → wide) to simulate a two-camera setup. Use on key statements and emotional beats. Turns choppy cuts into intentional angle changes. |
| 5 | **B-roll coverage** | No 60 straight seconds of talking head. Even 1–2 s overlays (hands, objects, screen recordings, relevant footage) while audio continues — the news-broadcast principle. |
| 6 | **Pace variation** | Cut cadence matches energy: hype content 1–2 s cuts; emotional/storytelling breathes at ~5 s. Identical cut lengths become predictable and the brain tunes out. Never rapid-fire a vulnerable moment; never slow-cut a hype clip. |
| 7 | **Pattern interrupts** | Once a viewer predicts the rhythm, they leave. Break your own pattern at least every 7–10 s: a sudden zoom, color shift, unexpected cut, tone change. |
| 8 | **Music sync** | Cuts land on beat drops/transitions. Mark beat points before cutting. On-beat edits read as professional; off-beat reads amateur. Monetized placements: royalty-free or original audio only. |
| 9 | **Hook–payoff connection** | The ending must deliver exactly what the opening implied. A missing or mismatched payoff tanks completion AND trains the algorithm that the account's hooks are clickbait. |
| 10 | **CTA placement** | Follow/comment/link CTA in the final 15–20%, after the value — never before. Premature CTAs break immersion. |

## Caption & graphic standards (mandatory, alongside the 10)

- **Captions on every clip** — roughly 85% of social video is watched muted; captions are also a retention tool and pseudo-SEO (platform text recognition reads them).
- **Brand-locked styling** — the creator's brand-kit font and two colors (one normal, one emphasis — Wk 6 brand kit), never editor's choice or app defaults. Caption style is part of the one-second recognizability test.
- **Key words emphasized** — bigger, emphasis color, or a pop/shake on the words that matter.
- **Center 80% of frame** — platform UI covers the edges; verify placement with an in-app draft check before going live.
- **Every graphic justifies itself AND carries a sound effect** — if a visual doesn't communicate or enhance, it's a distraction: cut it. If it stays, it gets a whoosh/pop/thud (contextual to the topic for bonus reinforcement) — sound makes the graphic feel embedded, not floating.

## Audio mix spec

| Layer | Level |
|---|---|
| Voice | −6 to −3 dB — loudest element in the mix |
| Background music | −18 to −12 dB — felt, not heard |
| Sound effects | −12 to −6 dB — varies by purpose |
| SFX density | 3–5 per 30 s of video — produced, not over-edited |

**Triple-playback QC (last step, every clip):** listen with headphones, without headphones, and on a phone speaker. Good on all three = ship. Also re-watch once fully muted — the clip must still work silent.

## Production benchmarks (the bar editors are held to)

| Benchmark | Value |
|---|---|
| Edit time cap | ≤1 hour per clip in CapCut (simple trend clip ≈ 15 min) |
| Quality bar | 7/10 at scale, first-draft-publishable — not 10/10 once a week |
| Batch economics | ~21 clips ≈ 3 weeks of content ≈ 40 man-hours total across scripter/editor/creator |
| Testing discipline | Topic strict, format varied — test formats inside the niche, never off-topic (protects Wk 1 algorithm match) |
| Outcome metric | Account AWT (Wk 1 grading) — hold >3 s, chase the 10 s gold standard |

Benchmarks are from the course's capstone case study — treat as working targets, tightened against our own data.

## Escalation

Clip fails the same checkpoint twice → head editor review with the editor same day. An editor repeatedly over the 1-hour cap or under the 10/10 pass → pairing session on the checklist, then capacity/fit conversation (editor hiring standard, ops appendix §1). Any clip found published without passing QC → trace how it bypassed Ready to Schedule and close the gap (pipeline SOP escalation).
