Edit for retention — what happens to your clip after you hit upload

The finale: the pipeline every clip you shoot moves through, the 10-point standard it must pass, and the recycling system that keeps your best content earning for you forever.

The idea

The algorithm pays for exactly one thing: retention — what percentage of viewers make it to the end. So everything our editors do to your footage is retention editing: every cut, caption, zoom, and sound is a deliberate choice that keeps a viewer around a few seconds longer. Three minutes of raw footage goes in; about 45 seconds comes out. That's not waste — that's the job. Every pause, every breath, every moment of dead air gets cut, because even half a second of nothing tells a viewer "this part isn't for me."

Just as important is where your clips live. Every creator at ALTR runs on five folders:

FolderWhat happens there
1 · DumpYou drop everything straight off your phone the day you shoot. Zero friction — shoot, dump, done.
2 · Needs EditingWe triage by turnaround: high = now · medium = 72 hours · low = within a week.
3 · Ready to ScheduleEdited clips that passed the 10-point standard, waiting for the scheduler.
4 · ScheduledFiled by month and week of posting date — so we always know when a clip last ran.
5 · Greatest HitsProven winners, re-posted roughly every 30 days. Free volume from content that already paid for itself.

Over time the Greatest Hits folder becomes a literal map of what your audience wants most — and it's why we never delete raw footage: today's reject is next month's B-roll, hook test, or brand-new post.

This week's trick Your entire job in post-production is two sentences: get every raw clip into your Dump folder the same day you shoot it — and never delete anything. The pipeline does the rest.

The 10-point standard every clip must pass

CheckWhy it holds viewers
1 · First-frame motionA static opening gets swiped — movement in frame one triggers curiosity first.
2 · Re-hook every 3 sAttention decays in ~3 s cycles; a cut, text pop, or sound restarts the clock.
3 · Zero dead airPauses, ums, and breaths are cut. 3 min raw → 45 s posted.
4 · Punch-insAlternating 10–20% zooms turn one phone into a "two-camera" shoot.
5 · B-roll coverageThe frame keeps changing even while the audio runs on.
6 · Pace matches energyHype cuts every 1–2 s; emotional moments breathe at ~5 s.
7 · Pattern interruptsEvery 7–10 s the edit breaks its own rhythm — no predictability.
8 · Cuts sync to beatsCuts landing on the music feel intentional, not choppy.
9 · Hook pays offThe ending delivers what the opening promised — no clickbait debt.
10 · CTA in final 15–20%The ask lands after the value, where viewers are most satisfied.

On top of the ten: captions always on — roughly 85% of social video is watched muted — in your brand's locked style, inside the center 80% of the frame; every graphic justifies itself and carries a sound effect; audio mixed voice-first and checked on headphones, speakers, and a phone before anything ships. And edits are capped at about an hour — strong 7/10 content at scale beats perfect content once a week.

This week's homework

  1. Dump every raw clip from the last month into your Dump folder — especially the ones you almost deleted.
  2. With your ALTR manager: pick three Greatest Hits from your last 90 days and put them back on the schedule.
  3. Watch your newest edited clip muted. If it works silent, it works — that's how most people will see it.
