# WK 04 — Pick a Niche You Can Win (APE/TAM/DRR + the Trucker Test)

> Drop date: Dec 14, 2026 · Source lessons: 2.5 (choosing the best path), 2.6 (practical framework) · KB 2.3a / 2.3b / 2.3c

## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Stop competing with every pretty girl on the internet — find the specific territory where you can be the one creator your audience recognizes as their own, and prove it's big enough to build a business on before you commit.

## The idea

Most creators in this space think "niche" means their most noticeable physical trait. That worked in the early days, when demand massively outran supply — that era is over. Every looks-based lane is saturated, and AI-generated creators will flood them further the moment there's money in it. The only moat nobody can copy is a **genuine, differentiated personality inside an interest-based niche** the algorithm can identify and serve (this is the matchmaker from week 1 again — it can't match "pretty" to anyone).

Think in three tiers. **Category** — fitness, gaming, cooking: millions of creators, billions of viewers; you cannot compete here, and the algorithm can't target it. **Niche** — powerlifting, retro gaming, southern cooking: defined enough that the algorithm has a target. **Subniche** — women's beginner powerlifting, Louisiana Creole cooking: specific enough that your audience recognizes you as one of their own, with enough demand left to build a business. Scale check: a decade ago "Minecraft content" genuinely was a niche; today it's an industry with 200M+ players — calling it a niche is like calling Brazil a neighborhood.

How do you know you've drilled deep enough? The **Trucker Test** from last week: a subniche is specific enough when you can describe the *one specific fan* it serves — his age, his job, what he listens to, why he's scrolling at 1 a.m. If your "niche" could describe half of Instagram, keep drilling.

Then the viability gut check, three letters at a time — **APE / TAM / DRR**:

- **APE** (authenticity · passion · expertise): the easiest audience to reach is the one you belong to. Could you talk about this on camera for hours without a script? Passion can be coached; boredom can't be hidden.
- **TAM** (total addressable market): a rough audience estimate of **~3–7M (rule of thumb: ~5M)** is the sweet spot — big enough to build a business, small enough to own. Cooking at large is over a billion Instagram users — untargetable. The course's worked example: Mormon home cooking, est. 3–7M, and thoroughly American (Tier-1 audience by default).
- **DRR** (difficulty · resources · replicability): can you produce it 1–3×/day without burning out or going broke? A kitchen works; skydiving without a plane doesn't. The core activity must be endlessly iterable — and supplementable with personality content around it.

Finally, **reverse benchmarking**: don't copy the top account in your subniche — that's fighting an incumbent on their home turf. Instead, list the top creators, pull their all-time best content (~250 top Reels), and *read the comments*. Comment sections tell you what the audience is asking for that nobody is making. That gap is your positioning. And benchmark **outside the adult space**: creators who win without an attractiveness advantage have provably good content. Attractiveness is a multiplier, not a strategy — find content that wins on merit, then add your advantage.

## This week's trick (the single imperative)

Write your three tiers — category, niche, subniche — and score the subniche on APE/TAM/DRR. If you can't describe the one specific fan it serves, you're still at category level: drill until you can.

## Numbers that matter

| Metric | Benchmark | Source |
|---|---|---|

## Do / don't

**Do:** pick interest + personality over physical traits — traits are a multiplier, not a niche · benchmark creators outside the adult space who win on content merit, then add your advantage · read competitor comment sections — audiences write your roadmap for free.
**Don't:** stay at category level ("fitness girl" is invisible to the matchmaker) · chase the biggest market — the biggest is the most contested; ~5M you can own beats 500M you can't · copy the #1 account's content — reverse benchmarking finds what they're *missing*, not what they're doing.

2. With your ALTR manager: get a rough TAM estimate for your subniche. Are you near the sweet spot, or still at Brazil-is-a-neighborhood scale?
3. Find three creators **outside the adult space** winning in your subniche. Save their five best posts each — that's your study set, and we'll mine it together.
