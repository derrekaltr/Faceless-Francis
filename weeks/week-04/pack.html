Pick a niche you can win — not the biggest one

"Hot" is not a niche, and neither is "fitness". This week: the three-tier drill-down and the three-letter gut check that find the territory you can actually own.

The idea

Most creators treat their most noticeable physical trait as their niche. That era is over. Every looks-based lane is saturated, and AI-generated creators will flood them further. The only moat nobody can copy is a genuine, differentiated personality inside an interest-based niche the algorithm can serve. (Week 1's matchmaker can't match "pretty" to anyone.)

Think in three tiers. Category — fitness, gaming, cooking: millions of creators; you can't compete here. Niche — powerlifting, southern cooking: now the algorithm has a target. Subniche — women's beginner powerlifting, Louisiana Creole cooking: specific enough that your audience recognizes you as one of their own. ("Minecraft" was a niche in 2009; today it's an industry of 200M+ players — calling it a niche is like calling Brazil a neighborhood.)

How deep is deep enough? Last week's test: can you describe the one fan this subniche serves — his age, his job, what he listens to, why he's scrolling at 1 a.m.? If it could describe half of Instagram, keep drilling.

This week's trick Write your three tiers, then score the subniche below. Can't describe the one fan it serves? That's still category level — drill until you can.

Trick of the WeekWK 04

Steal the gaps, not the content

Copying the biggest account in your subniche means fighting an incumbent on their home turf. We do the opposite — a trick borrowed from the advertising world called reverse benchmarking: list the top creators in your subniche, pull their all-time best posts (~250 top Reels), and read the comments. Comment sections tell you exactly what the audience is asking for that nobody is making. That gap is your positioning.

And benchmark outside the adult space. Creators who win without an attractiveness advantage have provably good content — study them, borrow what works on merit, then add your advantage on top.

"Attractiveness is a multiplier, not a strategy. Find content that wins on its own merit — then add your advantage."

Do / don't

Do

  • Pick interest + personality over physical traits — traits are a multiplier, not a niche.
  • Benchmark creators outside the adult space who win on pure content merit.
  • Read competitor comment sections — audiences write your roadmap for free.

Don't

  • Stay at category level. "Fitness girl" is invisible to the matchmaker.
  • Chase the biggest market — biggest means most contested. ~5M you can own beats 500M you can't.
  • Copy the #1 account. Reverse benchmarking finds what they're missing, not what they're doing.

This week's homework

  1. Write your three tiers on one piece of paper. Stuck at category? Write three things you could talk about for two hours straight — your niche is hiding in there.
  2. With your ALTR manager: get a rough audience-size estimate for your subniche. Near the sweet spot, or still Brazil-is-a-neighborhood scale?
  3. Find three creators outside the adult space winning in your subniche. Save their five best posts each — we'll mine them together.
