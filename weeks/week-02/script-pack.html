Body

Think about ketchup. Nobody buys the objectively best ketchup. They grab the one they feel they know — and that brand sells seventy percent of the ketchup in America without being the tastiest or the cheapest. When every product on the shelf is basically the same, brand is the whole game. And in this business, every offer on the shelf is basically the same.

Your brand is three things. One — recognizability: someone sees a single second of your content and knows it's you. Two — association: seeing you triggers a feeling. Three — identity: your people look at you and think, "she's one of us." Which means — and this is important — you are not for everyone. On purpose.

GFX: six stages appearing as a ladder

Now, here's how a stranger becomes a paying fan. Six stages. Discovery — the algorithm shows you to them. Recognition — they start noticing you. Familiarity — they know your name and your vibe. Attachment — they feel like they know you. Loyalty — they defend you in your comments, they share you, they show up every post. And monetization — they pay.

Here's the part almost nobody gets right: most creators stall in the exact same place — between familiarity and attachment. The audience knows who you are. They just don't feel anything yet. Views look fine. Nobody clicks.

B-ROLL: scrolling a comment section

And your comments will tell you precisely where you are. Fire emojis and nothing else? That's recognition. They use your name and your jokes, but never click? Familiarity — the stall. Personal stories, "this made my day," DMs picking up? That's attachment — now it's working.

So this week's play: diagnose your stage honestly, then aim every post at the next stage — not at the sale. If they're at familiarity, don't push the link. To someone who doesn't feel connected yet, a link is just a stranger asking for money.

Homework + close

Your homework: read your last fifty comments and tell me your stage. Then post one thing this week designed purely to make people feel like they know you. No promotion. Just connection.
