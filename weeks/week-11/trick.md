# WK 11 — Shoot Like a Studio (Shots, Angles, Lighting, Directing on Set)

> Drop date: Feb 1, 2027 · Source lessons: 4.7 (shots & angles), 4.8 (lighting fundamentals), 4.9 (cinematic lighting), 4.10 (practical setups), 3.9 (directing with clarity + respect) · KB 4.3a–4.3d / 4.4a

## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Make every frame you shoot look deliberately, professionally made — on the phone you already own, with about $10 of extra gear — and know exactly how a directed ALTR shoot day works.

## The idea

Before you say a word, the frame has already spoken. Viewers decide in milliseconds whether content reads as professional — and a frame that looks like cinema stops the scroll all by itself, before the hook even lands. Composition (what's in the frame and where) plus light (how it's filled in) do that work. In a 9:16 vertical frame this is simpler than it sounds, because there are only three decisions: **the shot, the angle, the light.**

**Shot distance = importance.** Seven standard shots, from extreme close-up (one detail fills the frame — intimacy, a teased reveal, first-frame curiosity) through close-up, medium close-up, waist-up, cowboy (mid-thigh), full body, to wide (the setting becomes the character). You pick the distance by what the viewer needs to feel. And the background is your introduction: if the set says who you are — the gaming rig, the gym, the kitchen — you never waste your opening line on "Hi, I'm…". The frame handles the intro so your first words can hook.

**Camera angle = status.** Shot from slightly below, the audience looks up at you — elevated, aspirational, in charge. From above, looking down — softer, more vulnerable, more intimate. Eye level — a peer talking to a friend. None of the three is wrong; the mistake is letting the tripod decide instead of you.

**Light fills the outline.** If a shot feels flat, harsh, or "off" in a way you can't name, it's almost always the light. Two rules govern everything: **expose for the face** (if the face looks right, everything else can be brighter or darker *on purpose*) and **motivate the light** (it should appear to come from a source the viewer can believe — the window side, the lamp side). Then climb the setup ladder: one key light at 45°, slightly above eye level, angled down (with a ~$10 white bounce board as fill) → add a hair light behind and above (the biggest visual upgrade per dollar) → full 3-point with a fill at 50–70% of the key.

This is also the standard your ALTR team runs on a **directed shoot day**: a shot list built from your content bank, a 30-second frame check before every setup, and direction that always says *what* to change and *why it performs* — specific, respectful, never "do it again."

## This week's trick (the single imperative)

No frame is an accident this week: before every record press, call three things out loud — the shot, the angle, and where the light is coming from (and whether it looks like it comes from there).

## Numbers that matter

| Number | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 45° | Key light angle off camera, slightly above eye level, angled down | 4.10 |
| 1/(2× framerate) | Shutter speed rule — 24 fps → 1/50, 60 fps → 1/120, then leave it | 4.8 |
| 50–70% | Fill intensity relative to key — shadows soften, never vanish | 4.10 / breakdown |
| ~$10 | Bounce board ("free second light") · Moment app for iPhone manual exposure | 4.10 / Live Session 8 |
| 3000–6000K | Consumer LED range — covers almost every scenario; warm = intimate, cool = clinical | 4.9 |

## Do / don't

**Do:** lock exposure manually (Moment app, ~$10) — phone auto-exposure drifts mid-take · set shutter to 1/(2× framerate) and leave it alone · match color temperature across all lights · shoot near a window at 45° — the best free soft light there is (warm mornings/evenings, harsh cool midday).
**Don't:** put the key light dead-center behind the camera (flat passport-photo light) · run the fill as bright as the key (flat office light) · blow out highlights on the face — or let a white wall blow out behind you · leave ceiling lights on to fight your key.

## This week's homework (client actions)

1. Film the same clip in three shot types and at least two angles. Watch them back to back — the feel changes before the content does.
2. Build the one-light look: key at 45°, slightly above eye level, overheads off, white board opposite. Shoot this week's content on it.
3. Scroll your feed for five minutes and find three posts where the light feels wrong. Can you name why? (Once you see motivation, you can't unsee it.)
