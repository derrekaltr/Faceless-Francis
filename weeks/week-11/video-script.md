> Setting: clean branded set (charcoal backdrop, mint accent) · Framing: eye level (relatable-expert) — and this week the set itself is the demo: key at 45°, hair light on

## Script

**COLD OPEN (hook, ≤5s)**

That creator whose content looks like a movie? Same phone as yours. The difference costs about ten dollars — and three decisions.

**INTRO**

I'm [NAME], your social media director at ALTR — week eleven. You know what to post, when to post, and what to say. This week: making every frame look like we hired a film crew. Without hiring a film crew.

[GFX: "WK 11 — SHOOT LIKE A STUDIO"]

**BODY**

Here's the thing about the feed: before you've said a single word, your frame has already told the viewer how professional you are — and their brain decides in milliseconds. A frame that looks like cinema stops the scroll all by itself. So let's make three decisions on purpose.

[GFX: SHOT · ANGLE · LIGHT]

Decision one: the shot. Distance is importance. Fill the frame with your face when the moment is emotional or intimate. Cut at the waist when your hands and body language are doing work — stories, tutorials. Go full body when the outfit or the room is the point. Ask what the viewer needs to feel, then pick the distance that sells it.

[B-ROLL: same clip framed close-up / waist-up / full body]

Decision two: the angle. Angle is status. Camera slightly below you, audience looking up — elevated, aspirational. Camera above, looking down — softer, more intimate. Dead-on at eye level — a peer, talking to a friend. None of them are wrong. The mistake is letting the tripod decide instead of you.

Decision three — the big one: light. One light, or a window, forty-five degrees off to one side, a little above your eye level, angled down. That's how daylight falls, and it flatters everyone. Kill the ceiling lights fighting it. Shadow side too dark? A white poster board on the other side bounces light back. That board is the ten dollars.

[GFX: key at 45° + bounce board opposite]

Want the single biggest upgrade after that? A small light behind you, aimed at your hair and shoulders. That rim pulls you off the background like a magazine cover.

Last thing: expose for the face. If your face looks right, everything else can be bright or dark on purpose. Your phone's auto-exposure will drift mid-take trying to help — lock it manually; a ten-dollar app does it.

[B-ROLL: auto exposure vs locked, side by side]

And on a directed shoot day, this is exactly the checklist your ALTR team runs — shot list, frame check before every setup, and direction that tells you what to change and why. Same standard, your phone or ours.

**HOMEWORK + CLOSE**

Your homework is one clip, shot twice. Ten seconds, same content. Version one: however you shoot now. Version two: one light at forty-five degrees, overheads off, exposure locked on your face. Put them side by side — you'll never go back.

[GFX: "HOMEWORK: one clip, twice"]

Next week is the finale: editing for retention — the ten-point checklist our editors live by — and the full pipeline from raw clip to posted content. See you then.
