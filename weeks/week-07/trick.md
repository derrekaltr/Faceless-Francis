## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Learn why *who* watches you matters more than *how many* — and which popular growth tactics quietly fill your account with an audience that will never support you.

## The idea

Billboard Theory: a billboard seen by 10,000 people who want exactly what you offer is worth more than one seen by a million people who don't. Views are not the goal — the *right* views are. A video that reaches 10,000 members of your exact target audience outperforms a million random views every time, because audience members convert and random viewers don't.

This has a hard consequence: **some growth is bad growth.** Certain tactics reliably inflate your numbers with viewers who will never become fans — and worse, they teach the algorithm (Week 1) to keep finding more of the same wrong people. The big three offenders the course names: generic thirst-trap content (it needs no shared language or culture, so it attracts everyone and connects with no one), paid shoutouts and follow-swaps (you inherit someone else's mismatched audience), and chasing viral reach outside your niche.

The good news: your content *is* your audience filter. Every choice — your humor, your cultural references, the music you use, the setting, the in-jokes — either draws your people closer or waters your audience down. Content that *requires* your audience's context to enjoy (their slang, their interests, their world) screens out everyone else automatically. That's not a bug; it's the whole mechanism. A video only your people fully get is a video that builds an account of only your people.

## This week's trick (the single imperative)

Audit your last month: find the posts that pulled your biggest numbers, and honestly ask whether they pulled your *people* — or just a crowd. Then stop feeding the crowd.

## Why audience quality shows up in the money

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| High views, low saves/comments/DMs | A crowd, not your people — numbers that won't convert |
| Comments in your audience's own language | Right people — the filter is working |
| A viral post followed by weaker reach | A mismatched hit contaminated your next test groups (Week 1) |
| Followers climbing, link clicks flat | Growth without fit — the classic bad-growth signature |

## Do / don't

**Do:** make content that needs your audience's context to land · read your comments for whether the *right* people showed up, not just how many · let narrowness do the filtering — it's supposed to exclude people.
**Don't:** buy followers or trade shoutouts for raw numbers · post generic thirst-bait to juice views (it attracts everyone and connects with no one) · judge a post by views alone — judge it by whether your people engaged.

## This week's homework (client actions)

1. Pick your three highest-view posts from the last month. For each, check saves, comments, and DMs — did your *people* react, or just a crowd?
2. Read 20 recent comments. Are they in your audience's language, or generic? That tells you who you're actually attracting.
3. Kill one "bad growth" habit this week — a shoutout swap, a generic trend, a thirst-bait post — and put that slot into content only your people would love.
