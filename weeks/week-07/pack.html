Audience quality — the right 10,000 beats a random million

Who watches you matters more than how many. This week we find the "growth" that's quietly working against you.

The idea

Picture a billboard. One seen by ten thousand people who want exactly what you offer beats one seen by a million people who don't. Views aren't the goal — the right views are. Reach ten thousand of your exact audience and you'll out-earn a million random viewers every time, because your people convert and a crowd doesn't.

Which leads to a hard truth: some growth is bad growth. A few popular tactics reliably inflate your numbers with people who will never become fans — and worse, they teach the algorithm from Week 1 to keep finding more of the same wrong people. The three biggest offenders:

TacticWhy it fills you with the wrong audience
Generic thirst-baitIt needs no shared language or culture, so it attracts everyone and connects with no one.
Shoutouts / follow-swapsYou inherit someone else's mismatched audience — numbers up, fit down.
Off-niche viral chasingOne wrong hit contaminates your next several posts' test groups.

The good news: your content is your filter. Your humor, references, music, setting, in-jokes — each one either pulls your people closer or waters the audience down. Content that requires your audience's context to enjoy screens out everyone else automatically. A video only your people fully get builds an account of only your people.

This week's play Audit your last month: find the posts that pulled your biggest numbers, and honestly ask whether they pulled your people — or just a crowd. Then stop feeding the crowd.

Reading the signals

What you seeWhat it means
High views, low saves/comments/DMsA crowd, not your people — numbers that won't convert.
Comments in your audience's own languageRight people. The filter is working.
A viral post, then weaker reachA mismatched hit contaminated your next test groups.
Followers climbing, link clicks flatGrowth without fit — the classic bad-growth signature.

Do / don't

Do

  • Make content that needs your audience's context to land.
  • Read your comments for whether the right people showed up — not just how many.
  • Let narrowness filter for you. It's supposed to exclude people.

Don't

  • Buy followers or trade shoutouts for raw numbers.
  • Post generic thirst-bait to juice views — it attracts everyone, connects with no one.
  • Judge a post by views alone. Judge it by whether your people engaged.

This week's homework

  1. Take your three highest-view posts from the last month. For each, check saves, comments, and DMs — did your people react, or just a crowd?
  2. Read 20 recent comments. In your audience's language, or generic? That's who you're actually attracting.
  3. Kill one bad-growth habit this week — a shoutout swap, a generic trend, a thirst-bait post — and give that slot to content only your people would love.
