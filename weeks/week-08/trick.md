# WK 08 — Posting Cadence & the 5-Stage Scaling Framework

> Drop date: Jan 11, 2027 · Source lessons: 3.5 (scaling concepts), 3.6 (five-stage framework), 3.B (audience) + Live Session 3 (posting mechanics) · KB 3.3a–b / bonus

## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Know exactly when your posts go out and why, how much to post at every level, and the five stages ALTR uses to scale an account from one platform into an ecosystem — without ever letting quality slip.

## The idea

The number one scaling mistake in this space is growing *presence* before *quality*. The most efficient factory in the world doesn't matter if what it makes isn't worth buying. So ALTR scales like you'd set fishing lines: one line in the water, properly set, before the next one goes in. Three concepts drive everything:

- **Exposure** — how often a potential fan sees your content. More surface area, more chances to connect.
- **Content minutes** — a fan needs roughly 15–20 minutes of accumulated watch time before they trust you enough to pay. Every post is minutes in the bank.
- **The ecosystem** — if someone sees you every time they open Instagram, that's good. If they see you on Instagram, TikTok, X, *and* YouTube, the minutes pile up several times faster. That's why everything we make gets cross-posted everywhere: free extra reach for zero extra creative effort.

The machine also has settings we don't improvise. Posts go out at **9am, 12pm, and 3pm ET** — 9 and 12 are the strongest slots. Uploads are **1080p, not 4K** — platforms compress 4K badly and your crisp footage turns to mush. Output holds the 7/10-quality bar at 1–3 posts/day, and carousels are capped at **one per day, forever**. Your weekly grades (AWT · VTFR · HPF, from Week 1) are what tell us when volume can climb — and the same content signals that win your niche (language, humor, music, setting) are how we steer your audience toward the fans most likely to support you.

Two force multipliers as you scale: **use your sawdust** — every piece of content is raw material for more (a shoot becomes Reels, stills become carousels, a long cut becomes clips; like a lumber mill, nothing goes to waste) — and **repost your Greatest Hits** roughly every 30 days. Proven winners are free volume most creators never collect.

## This week's play (the single imperative)

Fill all three posting slots on schedule, every day, at your current stage's volume — and never add volume until the current level is steady at quality. More, posted worse, is less.

## How accounts scale — five stages

| Stage | What happens | The gate up |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Foundation** | One account per platform; 7 Reels + 7 carousels/week, built for your main platform, cross-posted everywhere | Comfortable at quality, every week |
| **2 · Posting volume** | 2–3 Reels/day; carousels stay at 1/day forever | Daily volume steady, grades healthy |
| **3 · Account volume** | Main account plateaus → launch a second account with a new format; collab-tag every post with the main + daily story pointing fans to it (the course's case study: ~90K followers in 3 weeks on a brand-new account) | Hitting diminishing returns on one account |
| **4 · Platform-specific** | Content made natively per platform, not just cross-posted | Years in; specialists hired |
| **5 · Full production** | Complete creative team — where the biggest creators in the world operate | Top fraction of a percent |

The gate between stages is **production capacity, never a follower count**. Scaling a weak formula just manufactures more of what doesn't work.

## Do / don't

**Do:** cross-post everything — free reach on every platform · squeeze derivatives out of every shoot (sawdust) · repost your proven winners ~every 30 days · let your weekly grades decide when volume climbs.
**Don't:** post past one carousel a day — there's no benefit beyond it · upload 4K · jump a scaling stage because a follower milestone feels close · fill slots with filler that drags you under the 7/10 bar.

## This week's homework (client actions)

2. Ask your manager which scaling stage your account is in and exactly what the gate to the next one is.
3. Find your best-performing post from more than 30 days ago — that's your first Greatest Hit going back into the schedule.
