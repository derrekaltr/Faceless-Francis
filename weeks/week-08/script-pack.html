Body

First, the settings. Your posts go out at nine, noon, and three Eastern — nine and noon are the strongest slots. And everything uploads in 1080p, not 4K. I know 4K sounds better. The platforms compress it so hard that your crisp footage turns to mush. 1080p survives.

Now — why does volume matter at all? One number: a fan needs roughly fifteen to twenty minutes of accumulated watch time before they trust you enough to pay. Every post is minutes in the bank. And if someone sees you every time they open Instagram, that's good — but if they see you on Instagram, TikTok, X, and YouTube, those minutes pile up several times faster. That's why every piece you make gets cross-posted everywhere. Free reach, zero extra work.

Here's how the climb works. Stage one: one account per platform, a Reel and a carousel every day. Stage two: two, then three Reels a day — and carousels stay at one a day, forever. There's no benefit past one. Stage three is my favorite: when the main account plateaus, we launch a second account with a brand-new format, tag every post as a collaboration with the main one, and point a daily story at it. In the course's case study, that took a brand-new account to ninety thousand followers in three weeks. Stages four and five — platform-specific content and a full production team — that's the long game.

And here's the rule over all of it: you move up when production can keep up. Never because a follower number says so. The most efficient factory in the world doesn't matter if what it makes isn't worth buying.

B-ROLL: content calendar filling slot by slot

One last multiplier. Use your sawdust. Every shoot becomes Reels, stills, carousels, clips — like a lumber mill, nothing goes to waste. And your proven winners? About every thirty days, they go back into the schedule. Free volume most creators never collect.

Homework + close

This week, one thing: find your best post from more than a month ago — the one your audience loved — and send it to your manager. That's your first Greatest Hit, and it's going back in.
