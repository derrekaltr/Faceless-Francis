# WK 09 — Hooks Win or Lose the Post (Two Formulas + the A/B Tournament)

> Drop date: Jan 18, 2027 · Source lessons: 4.1 (hook psychology), 4.2 (value hooks), 4.3 (entertainment hooks), 4.4 (testing & layering) · KB 4.1a / 4.1b / 4.1c / 4.2a

## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Stop guessing your first three seconds: write every hook from one of two proven formulas, then let a simple tournament tell you — in numbers — which openers earn you views.

## The idea

You're not competing against other creators for attention; you're competing against Netflix, sports, and everything else that eats free time. Reported research the course leans on says the average ability to focus on one task fell from ~150 seconds to ~47 between 2004 and 2020. The hook — your first 3–5 seconds — is the part of the post that earns the right to show the rest. Without it, the content never gets seen, however good it is.

Hooks work on brain chemistry: dopamine peaks in the **anticipation** of a reward, not the reward itself (you get the biggest hit smelling the food, not eating it). A great hook opens a gap — "I need to know what happens next" — and the viewer stays to close it. Which also means **hooks wear out**: audiences acclimate to anything they see repeatedly, the way tolerance builds to any stimulus. If your VTFR is sliding while your content hasn't changed, that's usually hook fatigue, not content decay — the fix is rotation, not a strategy teardown. It's also why "copy these 10 guaranteed hooks" lists die fast: mass use accelerates acclimation. Learn the formulas instead and you can write new ones forever.

**Formula 1 — value-driven content: Context → Promise Value → Contrarian Take (or open curiosity loop).**
Context filters for your exact viewer in one line ("is this about me?"). Promise value signals "I was where you are; I got the outcome you want." The contrarian take or open loop sideswipes an expectation so they can't leave. Example shape: *"Still doing an hour of cardio to stay lean? I quit a year ago — and got leaner. My secret? I eat more, not less."* All three parts present, in order.

**Formula 2 — entertainment content: (Context +) Premise → Anticipation → Subversion.**
Set the niche context instantly, establish a premise with tension, make the viewer anticipate a payoff, then subvert the expectation. The course's case study: a visually boring, mostly-static reel reportedly pulled 3M views on a 500-follower account purely because it nailed this structure (a second did 1.9M on 2,600 followers). The hook structure, not production value, did the work.

**The litmus test:** watch only the first 3–5 seconds of your video. Do you actually feel anticipation? If not, back to the drawing board.

## This week's trick (the single imperative)

Shoot the body once, shoot 3–5 different hooks for it, and start the tournament — from now on, hooks are promoted by numbers, not taste.

## Numbers that matter

| Number | What it means |
|---|---|
| 3–5 | Hook variants filmed per body — never just one |
| 2–3 days | Spacing between variants, posted over 1–2 weeks, identical captions & scheduled times |
| ≥ 0.5–0.7 VTFR | A hook earns a roster spot by holding this across rounds |
| ≤ 0.4 VTFR | Kill line — archive the hook, feed it to the "avoid" list |
| 5–10 | Target roster of proven hooks per account, rotated so none fatigues |

## Do / don't

**Do:** write the hook before you shoot, and check it against its formula part by part · film 3–5 hook variants per body (same body, new first lines) · rotate roster hooks so the audience never sees the same opener twice in a row · layer thoughtfully — visuals, text, movement, and sound should all reinforce one hook, not compete with it.
**Don't:** ride one winning hook until it dies — retire it while it's still working · copy viral hook lists ("guaranteed views" = fastest acclimation) · change captions or posting times mid-test (it corrupts the comparison) · judge a hook by one post — tournaments decide, rounds confirm.

## This week's homework (client actions)

1. Scroll your own feed for two minutes. Every time you stop, write down what stopped you. That list is your personal hook anatomy.
2. Write three hooks for your niche using Context → Promise Value → Contrarian Take. Label each part. Missing a part? It's not done.
3. Pick your best recent post, and with your ALTR manager film 3–5 new hooks for the same body. That's round one of your tournament — it starts this week.
