Body

Here's the psychology. Your brain releases the most dopamine right before a reward — the anticipation, not the payoff. Smelling the food, not eating it. A great hook opens a gap: "wait, I need to know what happens next." That feeling is the entire job of your first three seconds.

And there are exactly two formulas we use.

GFX: "CONTEXT → PROMISE → CONTRARIAN TAKE"

For value content: context, promise, contrarian take. Context tells your exact viewer "this is about you" in one line. The promise says "I was where you are, and I got the result you want." And the contrarian take flips something they believed — so they can't scroll away. If one of those three parts is missing, the hook isn't done.

GFX: "PREMISE → ANTICIPATION → SUBVERSION"

For entertainment content: set your world instantly, build a premise with tension, make them anticipate the payoff — then land somewhere they didn't expect. That's what the three-million-view video did. Structure, not production value.

Now, here's what nobody tells you: hooks wear out. Your audience acclimates to anything it sees repeatedly. So if your views are sliding and your content hasn't changed, that's usually hook fatigue — the fix is rotation, not panic.

B-ROLL: Edits app, swapping hook text over the same base video

Which brings me to the tournament. You already shoot one base video a day with us. From now on, we film three to five different hooks for the same body, post them a few days apart — same caption, same time, only the hook changes — and the numbers pick the winner. The winner faces new challengers next round. Keep going, and you end up with a roster of five to ten openers that are proven to work for your audience. Then we rotate them, and retire each one while it's still winning.

One last test before anything gets posted: watch just the first three to five seconds. Do you actually feel anticipation? If not — back to the drawing board.

Homework + close

Your homework: pick your best recent post, and write three to five new hooks for that exact same body using the formulas. Send them to your manager — round one of your tournament starts this week.
