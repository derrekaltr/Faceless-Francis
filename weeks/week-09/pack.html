Hooks win or lose the post — two formulas + the A/B tournament

Your first three seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest. This week: the two formulas every strong hook follows, and the tournament that promotes hooks on numbers, not guesses.

The idea

You're not competing with other creators for attention — you're competing with Netflix, sports, and everything else that eats free time. The hook is the part of your post that earns the right to show the rest. And it works on brain chemistry: dopamine peaks in the anticipation of a reward, not the reward itself — the biggest hit comes from smelling the food, not eating it. A great hook opens a gap ("I need to know what happens next") and the viewer stays to close it.

Which also means hooks wear out. Audiences acclimate to anything they see repeatedly — so if your view-to-follower ratio is sliding while your content hasn't changed, that's usually hook fatigue, not content decay. The fix is rotation, not a teardown. It's also why copied "guaranteed views" hooks die fast: everyone's audience has already seen them. Learn the formulas and you can write fresh ones forever.

The two formulas

Content typeFormula & example shape
Value-drivenContext → Promise value → Contrarian take (or open curiosity loop). "Still doing an hour of cardio to stay lean? (context) I quit a year ago — and got leaner. (promise) My secret? I eat more, not less." (contrarian) All three parts, in order — missing one means it's not done.
EntertainmentContext + Premise → Anticipation → Subversion. Set your niche instantly, build tension toward a payoff, then land somewhere the viewer didn't expect. The course's case study: a visually boring reel reportedly pulled 3M views on a 500-follower account purely on this structure.

The tournament

3–5hook variants filmed per body — never just one
2–3 daysapart, over 1–2 weeks · same caption, same posting time
5–10proven hooks on your roster at ≥0.5–0.7 VTFR · kill at 0.4

Each round, the best-performing hook wins and faces fresh challengers in the next one. Hooks that hold a healthy view-to-follower ratio across rounds join your rotating roster; hooks at 0.4 or below are retired for good. You already shoot one base video a day with ALTR — the tournament simply decides which first lines deserve to ride it.

"Watch only the first three to five seconds of your video. Do you actually feel anticipation? If not — back to the drawing board."

Do / don't

Do

  • Write the hook before you shoot — and check it against its formula, part by part.
  • Rotate roster hooks so no one sees the same opener twice in a row. Retire winners while they still work.
  • Layer thoughtfully: visuals, text, movement, and sound should all reinforce one hook — not compete with it.

Don't

  • Ride one winning hook until it dies — acclimation always wins eventually.
  • Change captions or posting times mid-test. If anything but the hook changes, the test is ruined.
  • Judge a hook on one post. Tournaments decide; rounds confirm.

This week's homework

  1. Scroll your own feed for two minutes. Every time you stop, write down what stopped you. That list is your personal hook anatomy.
  2. Write three hooks for your niche using Context → Promise value → Contrarian take. Label each part.
  3. Pick your best recent post and, with your ALTR manager, film 3–5 new hooks for the same body. Round one starts this week.
