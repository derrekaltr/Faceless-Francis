# WK 03 — One Specific Fan (Avatar, Their Language, Your Archetype)

> Drop date: Dec 7, 2026 · Source lessons: 2.3 (audience avatar), 2.4 (speaking their language), 2.B (psychosexual archetypes) · KB 2.2a / 2.2b / 2.BONUS

## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Define exactly who your content is for — one specific person, not "everyone" — learn to speak his language, and pick the character flavor that makes your brand unmistakably yours.

## The idea

**Billboard Theory.** Imagine you get one free billboard, anywhere on earth. Almost everyone puts it where the most people will see it — Times Square. Wrong. If the crowd walking past has no interest in what you offer, a million views are worth nothing; the right spot is wherever *your* people already gather, even if it's a truck-stop exit on the interstate. The goal is never the biggest audience — it's the best one. Ten thousand perfect fans beat a million randoms who will never pay.

This plugs straight into week one's matchmaker: the algorithm's only job is matching content to the audience most likely to engage. If *you* can't say who your content is for, the algorithm can't either — and it defaults to the wrong people, or to nobody. Knowing your audience isn't branding fluff; it's how the distribution machine finds your fans. ("Stuck at 200 views" is very often an avatar problem wearing a metrics costume.)

**The avatar standard (the "Trucker Test").** "Men who like pretty girls" describes half the species — that's a demographic, not a person. The standard is one specific, imaginable human at *subculture* granularity: he's 34, drives long-haul routes between Texas and Tennessee, listens to podcasts on the drive, hunts on weekends, scrolls his phone in a truck-stop parking lot when he can't sleep, has never paid for a subscription but has definitely thought about it. You know what his feed looks like and what makes him stop scrolling. Not every viewer must *be* him — but the clearer you picture him, the better the algorithm finds thousands more like him.

**SCV — Shared Cultural Vernacular.** Every niche has its own shorthand; gamers don't talk like frat boys, anime fans don't talk like cowboys. Using the insider word ("hit a PR on my deadlift") signals "one of us"; the outsider phrase ("lifted the heaviest weight ever") breaks the fantasy instantly — one wrong word and the audience smells a fake. The fix is immersion: watch the niche's content, read the comments, learn the slang and inside jokes.

**Archetypes — emotional niching.** Short-form can't carry a complex personality; every post must communicate your character in seconds, so we simplify into an archetype — one of 10 classic character flavors in 3 families (nurture / power / relational). Same avatar + same niche + different archetype = a completely different brand ("don't cheat on me, I know engines" vs. "tell me about your day while I change your oil"). It's a differentiating layer, not the whole brand — and the one rule is that your archetype must intersect your natural personality. A forced character reads as fake, and fake kills connection.

## This week's trick (the single imperative)

Write your one specific fan — one paragraph, so specific you'd recognize him at a gas station — then rewrite your bio and your next three captions in *his* language.

## Do / don't

**Do:** build the avatar as a named person you could picture at a gas station · immerse in the niche — watch, read comments, collect the slang · pick the archetype closest to who you naturally are, then layer it consistently into every post.
**Don't:** target "everyone" — identifying with everyone means mattering to no one · guess at niche vocabulary (one wrong word outs you) · perform an archetype that isn't you — the audience senses forced character work and disengages.
