One specific fan — why "everyone" is the worst possible answer

Who is your content for? This week we name him, learn to speak his language, and pick the character flavor that makes you unmistakable. Specific beats big — every time.

The idea

Imagine you get one free billboard, anywhere on earth. Almost everyone puts it where the most people will see it — Times Square. Wrong. If the crowd walking past has no interest in what you offer, a million views are worth nothing. The right spot is wherever your people already gather — even if that's a truck-stop exit on the interstate. The goal is never the biggest audience. It's the best one.

This plugs straight into week one's matchmaker: the algorithm's only job is matching content to the people most likely to engage. If you can't say who your content is for, the algorithm can't either — it defaults to the wrong people, or to nobody. "Stuck at low views" is very often an audience-definition problem wearing a metrics costume.

The avatar standard

"Men who like pretty girls" describes half the species — that's a demographic, not a person. The standard is one specific, imaginable human: he's 34, drives long-haul routes between Texas and Tennessee, listens to podcasts on the drive, hunts on weekends, scrolls his phone in a truck-stop parking lot when he can't sleep. He's never paid for a subscription — but he's definitely thought about it. You know what his feed looks like and what makes him stop scrolling. Not every viewer has to be him — but the clearer you picture him, the better the algorithm finds thousands more like him.

"You'd rather reach ten thousand of the perfect fan than a million randoms who will never buy."
This week's play Write your one specific fan — one paragraph, so specific you'd recognize him at a gas station — then rewrite your bio and your next three captions in his language.

Speak their language — SCV

Every niche has its own shorthand — we call it Shared Cultural Vernacular: the tribal signal that says "one of us." One wrong word breaks the fantasy — the audience smells an outsider instantly. The fix is immersion: watch the niche's content, read the comments, learn the inside jokes.

NicheInsider says…Outsider says…
Gym"Hit a PR on my deadlift today""Lifted the heaviest weight I've ever lifted"
Gaming"A creeper blew up my build""The green exploding guy"
Fishing"Topwater at first light""The spinny thing on my fishing pole"

Pick your archetype — emotional niching

Short-form can't carry a complex personality — every post must communicate your character in seconds. So we pick one of 10 classic character flavors in 3 families, and the one rule is it must match who you naturally are. Forced character work reads as fake, and fake kills connection.

FamilyThe flavors
Nurture
warmth & attention		Mothering — warm, unconditional · Smothering — intense, all-in ("I can't stop thinking about you") · Distant — guarded; affection is earned

Same fan, same niche, different archetype — a completely different brand: "don't cheat on me, I know engines" vs. "tell me about your day while I change your oil."

This week's homework

  1. Write your one specific fan — one paragraph, gas-station specific. If it could describe half the population, start over.
  2. List 10 words or references only your niche uses — and the outsider phrasing you're now banned from.
  3. With your ALTR manager: pick your primary archetype — the one that feels most like you.
