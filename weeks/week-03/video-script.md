## Script

**COLD OPEN (hook, ≤5s)**

Quick question: who is your content for? If your answer is "everyone" — that's exactly why strangers watch and nobody pays.

**INTRO**

I'm [NAME], your social media director at ALTR — welcome to week three. Last week: how a stranger becomes a paying fan. This week: deciding, on purpose, exactly *who* that fan is.

[GFX: "WK 03 — ONE SPECIFIC FAN"]

**BODY**

Let's play a game. You get one free billboard, anywhere on earth. Where do you put it? Almost everybody says Times Square — maximum eyeballs. And that's wrong. If the crowd walking past has zero interest in what you offer, a million views are worth nothing. The right spot is wherever *your* people already gather — even if that's a truck stop off the interstate. You'd rather reach ten thousand of the perfect fan than a million randoms who will never buy.

[GFX: billboard in Times Square → billboard at a truck-stop exit]

And this connects straight back to week one. The algorithm is a matchmaker. If *you* can't say who your content is for, it can't either — so it shows you to the wrong people, or to nobody at all.

So here's the standard we use. Not "men who like pretty girls" — that's half the species. One specific person. He's thirty-four. He drives long-haul routes between Texas and Tennessee. He listens to podcasts on the drive, hunts on weekends, and scrolls his phone in a truck-stop parking lot when he can't sleep. He's never paid for a subscription — but he's definitely thought about it. *That's* an avatar. You know what his feed looks like. You know what makes him stop scrolling. And the clearer you picture him, the better the algorithm finds thousands more just like him.

[GFX: avatar card filling in — age, routine, feed, scroll-stopper]

Step two: speak his language. Every niche has its own shorthand. In the gym niche you say "I hit a PR on my deadlift" — not "I lifted the heaviest weight ever." In gaming, a creeper is a creeper — not "the green exploding guy." One wrong word and the fantasy breaks — the audience smells an outsider instantly. So immerse yourself: watch the content, read the comments, learn the inside jokes.

[GFX: insider phrase vs outsider phrase, side by side]

And step three: pick your archetype — the emotional flavor of your character. The warm best friend. The hype girl in his corner. The guarded one whose attention has to be earned. Same audience, same niche, different archetype — completely different brand. One rule: pick the one closest to who you actually are. A forced character reads as fake, and fake kills connection.

[B-ROLL: the three archetype families from the weekly PDF]

**HOMEWORK + CLOSE**

One thing this week: write your one specific fan. One paragraph, so specific you'd recognize him at a gas station. Bring it to your check-in — your manager and I are building your whole persona document from it.

[GFX: "HOMEWORK: write your one specific fan"]

Next week: how to pick a niche you can actually *win* — and why "niche" is the most misused word in this business. See you then.
