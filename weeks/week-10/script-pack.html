Body

One concept changes everything: the supply gap. It's the difference between what your audience wants to watch and what creators are actually making. When a niche's content is dominated by one type of creator, that's your opening. You're not competing with those creators — you're offering the audience something they literally cannot get anywhere else. And often the gap is simply you: your energy, your take, on content nobody like you is making.

GFX: "SUPPLY GAP = what they want − what they're getting"

Now, the counterintuitive part. You don't find that gap on Instagram or TikTok — their search barely works. You find it on YouTube. YouTube search is built for this, the content is better, and the people searching there are your exact future audience, actively looking for your topic. And don't study other creators in this space — find great content first, then make it yours.

Video ScriptWK 10

Every strong video you find goes through four steps. One: name the format — ranking, explainer, hot take. The format, not just the topic. Two: list the variations. One gaming video — "all eighty-seven of them, explained" — is, in theory, eighty-seven short videos. Three: adapt it — what's the version only you can make? Four: log it immediately. If you think you'll remember it later, you won't.

GFX: four steps stacking — FORMAT → VARIATIONS → ADAPT → LOG

Everything lands in your content bank — a simple sheet you and your manager run together. The rule from now on: twenty shoot-ready ideas banked, at all times. Find one deep vertical — a format with a thousand possible versions — and that's roughly a year of content aimed straight at your people.

B-ROLL: content bank spreadsheet filling in · GFX: "20+ IDEAS BANKED — ALWAYS"

From there, scripts get easy, because a script is just instructions. Your set, your outfit, your format — those are fixed. The script only writes what changes: the hook makes a promise, the body delivers it with zero fluff, and the ending asks for a comment using your niche's own words — those comments tell the algorithm exactly who to show you to.

One more thing — carousels. One every day, never more. To your most engaged fans a carousel feels personal, almost like a midday photo drop — and it quietly converts. And a line we don't cross: some people in this industry fake DM screenshots for content. We don't. Real screenshots, with your permission — or none.
