Never run out of ideas — the supply gap and the content bank

Running out of content isn't a creativity problem — it's a research problem. This week we install the system: a bank that always holds 20+ shoot-ready ideas.

The idea

The concept that unlocks infinite ideas is the supply gap: the difference between what your audience wants to watch and what creators are actually making. When a niche's content is dominated by one type of creator, that's your opening — you're not competing with them, you're offering the audience something they literally can't get anywhere else. The gap isn't always demographic, either; sometimes it's tone, energy, or personality. The question is always: what is the audience clearly not getting from the creators who already exist?

The counterintuitive part: you don't research on Instagram or TikTok — their search is nearly useless for niched content. You research on YouTube: its search actually works, the content is higher quality, and the people searching there are actively looking for your exact topic. And don't study other creators in this space for a style to copy — find great content first, then make it yours.

Everything you find flows into your content bank — a per-creator sheet where every row is a shoot-ready idea. Find one deep vertical — a format with a thousand possible iterations — and you've banked roughly a year of content purpose-built for your exact audience.

This week's trick Run one YouTube research session with your ALTR manager: name your supply gap, extract ideas from the best videos you find, and bank 20 shoot-ready ideas. From this week on, the bank never drops below 20.

A script is a set of instructions

Your static elements — set, wardrobe, format — are fixed once in your brand kit. The script only writes what changes: the hook makes a promise, the body delivers it, the CTA asks for a comment in your niche's own keywords — those comments tell the algorithm who to show you to. Three body rules: no fluff — every line advances the story or earns the next seconds, or it's cut; an engagement change every 3–4 seconds, written in before filming; and deliver what the hook promised. AI can draft fact-based scripts — never your personality, edge, or comedy.

Carousels & captions

Carousels are the forgotten format: reported platform data puts them at ~1.4x the reach and ~3.1x the engagement of single images — every swipe is an algorithm signal. To your most engaged fans a carousel feels personal, closer to a midday photo drop than a produced Reel — which is why it converts. One per day, never more. Structure: hook slide (the only one the feed shows) → value slides (one idea each, ≤30–40 words) → CTA slide — and end every slide mid-thought, so the swipe finishes the sentence.

ALTR policy — stated plainly A popular industry tactic is faking DM or tweet screenshots for carousel content. We don't do it. Screenshot carousels on your account use real screenshots, shared with your permission — or we skip the format entirely.

Captions follow the same skeleton: a hook under 125 characters (where the feed truncates) → a short story → one CTA ending in a specific question. Hashtags: 5–10, mixed sizes, rotating sets — real but marginal. Never spend more time on the caption than the content.

Do / don't

Do

  • Research on YouTube, not your own feed — search intent beats scroll luck.
  • Break every strong video into variations and log them immediately.
  • Pick one deep vertical and ride it — depth beats novelty.

Don't

  • Copy other creators in this space — find great content first, then make it yours.
  • Fake screenshots. Real ones with permission, or none.
  • Let AI write your personality — facts yes, voice no.
