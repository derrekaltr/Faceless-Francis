## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Install the system that means you never again stare at the app thinking "I have nothing to post" — a content bank that always holds 20+ shoot-ready ideas, plus the script, caption, and carousel formulas that turn them into posts.

## The idea

Great execution can't save a weak idea — the idea is the single most important part of the content. And ideas are not a creativity problem, they're a research problem. The concept that unlocks it is the **supply gap**: the difference between what your audience wants to watch and what creators are actually making. When a niche's content is dominated by one type of creator, that's your opening — you're not competing with them, you're offering the audience something they literally can't get anywhere else. The gap isn't always demographic; sometimes it's tone, energy, or personality. The question is always: *what is the audience clearly not getting from the creators who already exist?*

The counterintuitive part: you don't research on Instagram or TikTok — their search is nearly useless for niched content. You research on **YouTube**. YouTube search actually works, the content is higher quality on average, and the people searching there are actively looking for your exact topic — a much truer picture of what your target audience wants. Don't study other creators in this space for inspiration either: find great content in your niche's real verticals first, then make it yours.

Every strong video you find goes through a **4-step extraction**: identify the format (ranking, explainer, hot take — the format, not just the topic) → list the variations (one gaming-lore video, "all 87 of them explained," is in theory 87 individual shorts) → adapt it to you (what's the version only you can make?) → log it immediately in your **content bank** — a simple per-creator sheet with an Inspiration tab (links) and an Ideas tab where every row is shoot-ready. Find one deep vertical — a format with a thousand possible iterations — and you've banked roughly a year of content purpose-built for your audience.

**Scripts** then become easy, because a script is just a set of instructions. Your static elements — set, wardrobe, format — are fixed once in your brand kit; the script only specifies what changes. Hook makes a promise, body delivers it, CTA asks for a comment using your niche's keywords (keyworded comments help the algorithm route you to more of your people). Three body rules: no fluff (every line advances the story, reinforces the hook's promise, or earns the next few seconds — or it's cut); an engagement change every 3–4 seconds written into the script *before* filming; and always deliver what the hook promised. AI is fine as a research and drafting assistant for fact-based formats — never for personality, edgy takes, emotional beats, or comedy.

**Carousels** are the forgotten format: reported platform data puts them at ~1.4x the reach and ~3.1x the engagement of single images, every swipe is an algorithm signal, and to your most engaged fans a carousel feels personal — closer to a midday photo drop than a produced Reel. That makes it the parasocial/conversion asset: one per day, never more. Structure: hook slide (thumbnail logic — it's the only slide the feed shows) → value slides (one idea each, ≤30–40 words) → explicit CTA slide, with every slide ending mid-thought so the swipe finishes the sentence. **Policy, stated plainly: a popular industry tactic is faking DM/tweet screenshots for carousel content. ALTR prohibits it — screenshot carousels use real screenshots shared with permission, or we skip the format.**

**Captions** follow the same skeleton: a hook under 125 characters (that's where the feed truncates), a short story, and one CTA ending in a *specific* question. Hashtags: 5–10 per post, mixed sizes, a few rotating sets — real but marginal (~10–15% of discovery). Never spend more time on the caption than the content.

## Do / don't

**Do:** research on YouTube, not your own feed · break every strong video into variations and log them the moment you find them · pick one deep vertical and ride it · post one structured carousel every day, every slide ending mid-thought.
**Don't:** study other creators in this space for a style to copy — find great content first, then make it yours · trust your memory ("I'll log it later" means you won't) · fake screenshots — real ones with permission, or none · let AI write your personality (facts yes, voice no).
