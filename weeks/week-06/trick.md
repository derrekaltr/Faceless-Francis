# WK 06 — Build the Brand Kit (One-Second Test, Wardrobe, Audio Identity)

> Drop date: Dec 28, 2026 · Source lessons: 2.8 (visual identity), 2.9 (audio identity), 2.11 (brand-build walkthrough) · KB 2.5a / 2.5b · `foundation/analysis-2.11-brand-build.md`

## The promise (one sentence, client-facing)

Walk away with a written brand kit — your look and your sound, decided on purpose — so a stranger glancing at your content knows who you are and what you're about in under a second.

## The idea

Recognition precedes everything. Week 2's pipeline showed that fans can't feel anything about a creator they don't recognize — and this week is where recognition gets engineered. The goal is to shrink the gap between "I think I've seen her pop up once or twice" and "oh, she dropped a new video" to as few exposures as possible.

Why week 6 and not week 1: **your audience and niche dictate your aesthetics**, not the other way around. You don't buy a whole wardrobe and then pick a direction. Now that your fan (Wk 3), your niche (Wk 4), and your formats (Wk 5) are locked, the look and sound follow from them.

Your **visual signature** has three levers. (1) *Physical features* — pick one or two defining features and lean into them; they're part of recognizability, but they're never the whole brand, and showing too much skin on socials makes you a target for bans. (2) *Wardrobe as costume* — the audience draws conclusions from what you wear whether you intend it or not, so be intentional: pick pieces, colors, and silhouettes that match the brand, then **repeat them until they're synonymous with you**. Steve Jobs wore the same black turtleneck every day — the uniform *was* the message. Wear a cowboy hat once and nobody remembers; wear it in every post and you're the cowboy-hat girl even to people who forget your name. (3) *Setting* — imagine your frame with you removed: it should still announce the niche. Warm lamps and overflowing bookshelves say one creator; pink LEDs and figurine shelves say another. One collectibles creator fills her shelf with the merchandise she unboxes — her niche registers before she says a word.

Your **audio identity** has three layers: *voice* (tempo, pitch, energy — lean into the voice you actually have and pick formats that suit it), *signature sounds and phrases* (the thing fans recognize before they even look up — including niche sounds, like a truck horn for a trucking creator), and *music*. On music the course is contrarian and we agree: **don't default to trending audio just because it's trending** — it makes you sound like everyone else. A curated shortlist of music your niche genuinely loves works as an in-group signal: fans feel the same jolt as meeting someone who loves their obscure favorite artist. One caveat worth knowing: audio steers *where* your viewers come from — a trending track from another country tends to pull that country's audience, so the playlist is also a targeting decision.

Everything above gets written into one document — your **brand kit** — in a working session with your ALTR manager. Written down, it does two jobs: on an off day you can slip into the character on command, and the team can reproduce your look and sound in every shoot, edit, and post without you re-deciding anything.

## This week's play (the single imperative)

Lock your uniform: one wardrobe formula, one setting, one signature prop, one music direction — written down in your brand kit — then repeat them in every post until recognition is automatic.

## The one-second test

Glance at a single frame of your content with the username covered. Can a stranger in your niche say **who this is** and **what it's about**? If yes for look *and* sound ("could you recognize it from across the room?"), the kit is working. If not, you're not building a brand — you're one more face in an ever-growing ocean.

| Kit element | Decided this week |
|---|---|
| Feature to emphasize | 1–2 defining physical features — accentuated, never the whole brand |
| Wardrobe formula | Pieces, colors, silhouettes + one signature piece worn every post |
| Setting | A backdrop that announces the niche with you out of frame |
| Signature prop | One repeatable object the frame is known for |
| Voice | The qualities to lean into — and the formats that suit them |
| Signature sound | A phrase or sound fans recognize before they look up |
| Music direction | A curated 10-track niche playlist, not default trending audio |

## Do / don't

**Do:** repeat your pieces relentlessly — once is invisible, every post is a brand · build a setting that tells the niche even without you in frame · curate a music shortlist your niche's in-group actually loves · write every decision into the kit so the team can reproduce it.
**Don't:** rotate outfits like a fashion haul — a hodgepodge of styles reads as nobody · make your body the entire brand, or push skin on socials (it invites bans, Wk 7) · grab trending audio by default — use a trend only when it genuinely reinforces your brand · pick aesthetics that fight your niche (cozy-book creator in a bright white gym-wear setup).

## This week's homework (client actions)

1. Run the one-second test: cover your handle and show a friend your last nine posts, one second each. Can they say who you are and what your niche is?
2. Pick your uniform — one wardrobe formula plus one signature piece or prop — and wear it in every post for the next two weeks.
3. With your ALTR manager: book your brand-kit session and bring a 10-track playlist draft plus three signature-sound ideas.
