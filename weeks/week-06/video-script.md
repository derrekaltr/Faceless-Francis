# WK 06 Video Script — Build the Brand Kit

> Presenter: ALTR social media director · Audience: ALTR client (creator) watching
> Runtime target: ~3 min (≈470 words of dialogue at ~140 wpm)
> Setting: clean branded set (charcoal backdrop, mint accent) · Framing: eye level (relatable-expert)

## Script

**COLD OPEN (hook, ≤5s)**

Cover your username and look at your last post. Would a stranger know it's you — in one second? If not, you're invisible.

**INTRO**

Hey — I'm [NAME], your social media director at ALTR. Week six. You know who your fan is, you've picked a niche you can win, you've locked your formats. This week we decide what you look like and what you sound like — on purpose.

[GFX: "WK 06 — BUILD THE BRAND KIT"]

**BODY**

Remember the pipeline from week two? Nobody can feel anything about a creator they don't recognize. Recognition comes first — and the game is shrinking the gap between "I think I've seen her once or twice" and "oh — she dropped a new video."

So we run everything through one filter: the one-second test. A stranger glances at a single frame — no username, no caption. Can they tell who you are and what you're about? That's the bar.

[GFX: frame with "WHO? + WHAT NICHE?" over a one-second timer]

Three things get you there. First, your features — pick one or two defining ones and lean in. Part of the brand, never the whole brand. Second — the big one — your wardrobe is a costume whether you treat it that way or not. So choose it once, then repeat it. Wear a cowboy hat in one video, nobody remembers. Wear it in every video, and you're the cowboy-hat girl — even to people who forget your name. Third, your setting. Picture your frame with you removed. It should still tell people the niche. Warm lamps and bookshelves say one thing. Pink LEDs and figurines say another. Pick the one that says you.

[B-ROLL: wardrobe rack, backdrop being dressed, signature prop placed]

Now close your eyes — because half of your brand is sound. Could someone recognize your video from across the room? Three layers. Your voice: don't fight it, lean into it. A signature sound or phrase fans know before they even look up. And music — here's where we're contrarian. Don't grab trending audio just because it's trending. It makes you sound like everyone else. A short playlist your niche actually loves is a handshake — it tells your people you're one of them. And audio even steers where your viewers come from: a trending track from another country pulls that country's audience. The playlist is a targeting decision.

None of this lives in your head. We write it down — your brand kit. One page: the uniform, the setting, the prop, the sounds, the playlist. On an off day, it's how you slip into character on command — and how my team makes every post look and sound like you.

**HOMEWORK + CLOSE**

This week, one thing: run the one-second test. Cover your handle, show a friend your last nine posts for one second each, and have them tell you who you are and what you're about. Bring their answers to your brand-kit session — that's where we lock your look and sound for good.

[GFX: "HOMEWORK: the one-second test — 9 posts, 1 second each"]

Next week: audience quality — why the right ten thousand followers beat a random million, and the "growth" tactics that quietly work against you. See you then.
