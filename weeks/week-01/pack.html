The algorithm is a matchmaker — and it's grading you on 3 numbers

How platforms actually decide who sees your content, and the three metrics ALTR tracks so your account grows on purpose, not by luck.

The idea

Platforms make money by keeping people scrolling, so the algorithm has one job: match every viewer with content they'll keep watching. It's a matchmaker. Help it make good matches, and it distributes your content to your exact future fans — for free.

When you post, the platform fingerprints the video — what it sees, what it hears, what your caption says — and predicts who will like it. Then it runs a test: it shows the post to a small group of roughly 200 people, mostly strangers, not your followers. Strong reaction → boosted to ~2,000, then ~20,000, then ~200,000. Lukewarm → re-tested on a fresh group. Weak → it stops.

So "stuck at 200 views" is almost never a shadowban. The content failed its test group. The fix isn't posting more or chasing trends — it's making more specific content for a more specific audience, so the matchmaker knows exactly who to send you to. And one off-niche viral post is a trap: it contaminates your test groups, and your next several posts pay for it.

This week's trick For the next two weeks, every single post serves the same narrow audience — no exceptions, no off-niche trends — while we grade your account on the three numbers below.

Do / don't

Do

  • Post 1–3×/day — each post is another chance to appear in the same viewer's scrolling session.
  • Prompt comments: take a clear stance or ask a question your niche has opinions about. Keyworded comments help the algorithm tag you.
  • Adapt trends into your niche — the trend is the delivery vehicle, your niche is the content.

Don't

  • Buy followers — fake followers poison every future test group. There is no recovery discount.
  • Jump on off-niche trends, however viral. One wrong viral hit hurts your next several posts.
  • Obsess over posting times, hashtags, or captions. Real, but marginal — window dressing.
"The algorithm explicitly rewards niching down. This isn't just brand advice — it's how the technology actually works."
